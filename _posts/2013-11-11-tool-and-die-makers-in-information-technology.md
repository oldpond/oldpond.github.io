So, who are the Toolmakers?  They are the people building languages, and they are the people building the <strong>tools</strong> to build and extend languages. People like Markus Voelter , with his presentation on The Art of Building Tools, and the challenge of extending languages, to create tools, to create new languages. Meta Programming.  I'm tempted to say meta-meta programming, but I'm not talking about theory here.  I'm talking about practice.  {Do you write the contract between module and components here?  Yeah, I'm mixin' it up.}

And then there are the Diemakers.  These are the framework creators, the plugin makers, the DSL writers.   People like Svetlana Isakova, bravely wading through two language filters to bring us Kotlin.  Designing languages where HTML is a first class citizen and plugin makers can be responsible for their own formatting. Mixing prose and code.  Doesn't that violate the principle of separation of content and formatting?  But content and formatting belong to the domain beyond this layer.  A framework, like the wonderful Chicago Boss framework for Erlang/OTP, allows us to deliver the finished product, a slick website or a wonderful piece of software to drive.  It's the software tool and diemakers who make this layer possible.  Engineers live here.  Gamemakers live here (this means you, Phil).  {Is this where you write the contract?}

There are a lot of new languages coming out now. This burst of creativity is clear evidence we are jumping into the next paradigm. It's starting with the flood of interest in new languages, and languages that have remained relatively unnoticed.  Functional programming and Object Oriented programming are dancing like crazy these days.  And things like F#, Go, and Scala are the outcomes.  Where does Engineering and Art collide?  A beautiful automobile?  A wonderful 3D development environment?  In the age of craftsmanship, these things could only be created how many times?  In the age of engineering, we can create these beautiful things over and over again.  And we can be original in our designs.

So, who are you?  A Toolmaker or a Diemaker?  You're out there; I can see you, and I'm learning the dance.
