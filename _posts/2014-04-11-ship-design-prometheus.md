When the film Prometheus by Ridley Scott opens a figure (presumably Prometheus himself) is about to give the gift of life to the world.  He drinks a mysterious black liquid which dissolves his body and presumably seeds the planet with human DNA.  You may recall from ancient Greek mythology that Prometheus wanted to give the gift of fire to mankind, and for that he was punished.  A more modern interpretation sees him as representing the lone scientist striving to improve the human condition, risking unintended consequences.  Mary Shelly subtitles her novel Frankenstein The Modern Prometheus, and here we can see the parallel with genetic research.  The Prometheus figure in the film appears more like a priest or acolyte than a scientist given that he sacrifices himself for some uncertain result.

He looks up to see his ship hovering, and it appears spheroid, almost oblate, in the first view, and then prolate as it leaves the planet.  The second design we see is that of the Scientific Exploratory Vessel Prometheus.  It's a beautiful design: highly complex, graceful, efficient, versatile, and rugged.  The third and final design is that of the planet-side ships explored by the team. These ships are u-shaped spheroids.  The question to ask is why are there two different ship designs associated with 'the Engineers'?

One possible explanation is that the alien race is not a mono-culture. The alien who sacrifices himself on the planet at the beginning of the film is not part of the culture belonging to the aliens David makes contact with on their mission. It's easy for the viewer to assume that all the alien humanoids encountered in the film are of the same race and culture because they appear to be identical in appearance.  Good and evil.  Spread of knowledge, suppression of knowledge.  Military vs exploratory ships.

Now, let's say that you could, through genetic manipulation, perfect the human body.  We not far off from it.  If two humans have the exact same genetic makeup, are they the same person.  This is one of the themes running through the film.  Is there more to life than DNA?  What if two pockets of human on different parts of the planet, or on different worlds even, had the exact same technology for perfecting DNA structures.  Would both societies hold the exact same world views?  Its one thing to observe the natural sciences when trying to understand living things, but what about the social sciences?  Philosophy, art, religion?
