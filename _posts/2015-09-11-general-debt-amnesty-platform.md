If Canadians are going to solve the various crises that face not only this nation, but many nations of the world, we are going to need a fresh platform, one that solves the financial and environmental crises together.  What would such a platform look like?

The current financial crisis, brought on by the collapse of the financial system in 2008, can be solved with making a small, fundamental change to the way we do banking.  Instead of creating money in private banks and borrowing it back, The Bank of Canada will create the money that the Federal Government needs to provide shared services to the people of Canada.  This would mean governments would never borrow money again.  There would be no deficits.  There would still be budgets of course;  people can only do so much work in one budget cycle.   But there would be no borrowing for governments, just spending based on the finite amount of services they can provide each cycle.

At the same time, another historically proven method for clearing the financial logjam we find ourselves in, is the general debt amnesty.  A debt amnesty is basically the act of declaring a debt null and void.  A general debt amnesty is the act of declaring all debts null and void.  You could also declare a middle ground where only government and personal debt is forgiven.  This type of debt amnesty would eliminate all credit card and mortgage debt on the personal side, and all municipal, provincial, and federal debt on the government side.

Since all government debt would be eliminated, and since the government would create its own money, all current and future governments would be debt free.  Individuals would find themselves out of debt and with quite a bit more disposable income.  They are of course free to carry on using credit cards, and they could certainly get mortgages and loans if they so desire.  A general debt amnesty would not eliminate these financial instruments, just zero them out.

What does this mean for the country in general?  It means we get a kick-start right when we need it the most.  The new government would have a never-ending demand for two very important shared services: roads and parks.  Since roads and parks belong to all the people of Canada, these will be fully funded federal services.  Of course, funding can flow to local governments as it does today where provinces and cities manage these areas locally.

There are some big changes happening in Canada right now, and making these adjustments to the financial system will enable us to deal with our future effectively.  We can now get oil from the Alberta tar sands.  That's a game changer.  The icecaps are melting.  That's a big one.  The baby boomers are all "hitting sixty".  These are tipping points in history.  Now is the time to make these fundamental changes that can be the foundation of future government.

Canada would build the best roads and most beautiful parks in the world.  Canada would restore wetlands, grow forests, clean rivers and revitalize lakes.  This would create strong demand in the natural sciences, forestry, engineering and architecture, to name just a few.  The downstream affect on our economic lives would be significant.

If we are going to build roads, then every inch of that road should be park.  If we are going to build factories, then they should be in parks.  If we are going to run rapid transit systems across the prairies, then it should be in the longest park in the world.  If we are going to run pipelines, then every inch of that line should be surrounded by the healthiest, most impact resistant ecosystem our industries can create.

That's the start of a good platform.
